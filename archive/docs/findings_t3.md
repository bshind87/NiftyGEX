## Why a raw hit rate is not evidence

The max-GEX strike usually sits very close to spot, so a 90-minute move often overshoots it. The no-effect hit rate is therefore well below 50% and depends on distance. Every test below compares the max-GEX strike with a comparator on the same days and the same price path. Only the strike differs.

The comparators:

- **Matched-distance mirror:** a strike at the same distance on the other side of spot. It is not a listed strike.
- **Snapped mirror:** the mirror moved to the nearest listed strike. Days where it lands on the max-GEX strike are dropped.
- **Distance-matched GEX rank 2–5:** the listed strike, among the 2nd- to 5th-largest GEX strikes, whose distance from spot best matches the max-GEX strike's.

The listed comparators remove a round-number explanation, because every strike they use is also a listed round strike.

## Reading

- **No attraction to the max-GEX strike against mirror comparators.** Overall and on expiry days, the max-GEX strike is not hit more often than the matched-distance mirror (Holm p 1.000 and 1.000) or the snapped listed mirror (Holm p 1.000 and 1.000).
- **The rank 2–5 rejection is not evidence for or against pinning.** The distance-matched GEX rank 2–5 comparison rejects under Holm, but in the *opposite* direction (max-GEX hit less often). The match is loose: median comparator distance is 0.298% of spot against 0.209% for the max-GEX strike, and farther strikes are overshot less.
- **When the match is tight, the difference disappears.** Restricted to matches within 0.1% of spot, hit rates are 0.414 vs 0.414 overall (p 1.000, 609 days) and 0.356 vs 0.319 on expiry days (p 0.675). As defined, the loose-match comparator in F3 is distance-confounded; the tight-match variant is the valid listed-strike test.
- **The close-strike effect is round-number attraction, not gamma.** For strikes within [0.000, 0.122]% of spot, the max-GEX strike beat the unsnapped mirror (0.207 vs 0.143, p 0.011). Against the snapped listed mirror it does not (p 0.731, 210 days). The mirror only looked worse because it is not a round strike.
