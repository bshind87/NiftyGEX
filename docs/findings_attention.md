# Attention maps (exploratory)

Generated by `scripts/report_attention.py` from the pooling weights stored by `scripts/run_neural.py --save-attention` (runs `8cfe1b261e53a1e3`, `e5cacfc5b84de69c`; eval config hash `552a3501001a`). A contract's relative weight is its pooling weight times the number of real contracts in that snapshot, so 1 means a plain average. Not part of the pre-registered tests.

## Findings

- Pooling attention is close to a plain average. At 15:15 the median effective share of contracts is 1.0000 for attention + GRU and 1.0000 for self-attention (1 = every contract weighted equally); the median largest relative weight is 1.008 and 1.006.
- Concentrated snapshots (effective share below 0.9 in at least one head) are rare: 4 of 1552 test days for attention + GRU and 2 for self-attention.
- The most concentrated day is 2026-03-10 (109 contracts): one put at log-moneyness -0.074 gets 33.8 times the average weight. Its OI changed by +6,628% since the previous close (day median +0.7%).
- On 4 of the 4 concentrated days for attention + GRU (and 2 of 2 for self-attention), the most-weighted contract is the day's largest OI change. This field is a percentage and is not log-transformed in the inputs, so a newly opened strike gives an extreme value that dominates the pooling; the planned field-drop ablation (run_neural.py --drop oi_change_since_previous_close_pct) tests whether it matters.
- Within a day, attention + GRU gives slightly more weight to calls (rank correlation +0.44) and less to high-volume, high-OI and high-|GEX| strikes (-0.30, -0.31, -0.30), but these tilts are small: binned by log-moneyness, the mean weight differs from a plain average by at most 0.5% (0.7% for self-attention).
- Reading: the pooling step adds little beyond averaging, which matches the RQ2 result that self-attention does not beat DeepSets. Interactions inside the encoder layers are not measured here; only the pooling weights are stored.
