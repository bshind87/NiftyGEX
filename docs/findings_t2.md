**Finding: the magnitude effect was confounding.** Once implied volatility, the expiry calendar and today's RV ratio are controlled for, the GEX-magnitude coefficient is no longer distinguishable from zero. Gamma is mechanically larger when IV is low and when expiry is near, and those variables, not dealer hedging per se, explain the VIX-only association. **Finding: the inferred sign adds information beyond magnitude; the naive sign does not.** When the Pro dealer proxy is net long gamma (positive inferred total GEX), the next-day RV ratio is lower, the direction the dampening hypothesis predicts. The effect holds before the weekly-expiry rationalisation marker but is not significant after it, on a much smaller post sample. The inferred sign comes from daily participant OI covering all index options, so it may proxy participant positioning rather than NIFTY gamma itself.

**Findings**

- **VIX alone has no skill**, because the target is relative to its own trailing level.
- **Untuned models are overconfident.** Their Brier score is no better than the class-frequency prior.
- **Regularization fixes most of the calibration.** It is chosen on a time-ordered inner validation split.
- **Platt scaling then makes the reference model beat the prior** on Brier in most walks.
