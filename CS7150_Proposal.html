1Problem and motivation

Option market makers hedge their books with futures, and the direction of that hedging depends on whether they are net long or net short gamma. If their aggregate position can be estimated from the public option chain, it should carry information about how the index will move.

On the S&P 500, practitioners summarise this with a single number, gamma exposure (GEX), and the idea that positive GEX suppresses volatility and pins prices near large strikes at expiry is widely repeated but thinly tested. India's Nifty 50 options market is now one of the largest in the world by contract volume, has frequent weekly expiries, and has a very different participant mix, so the S&P assumptions about which side dealers are on may simply not hold.

The deep learning question is whether a model that reads the whole option chain — every strike as a token — can learn a better positioning signal than the ~20 hand-built summary features practitioners use, and whether that learned representation stays useful when market rules change. Appendix A explains the finance concepts for readers new to options.

2Research questions

  1. RQ1Do Nifty expiry settlements cluster near high-gamma strikes or max pain more than a no-effect null predicts?Pinning hypothesis. Tested statistically; about 150–300 events.
  2. RQ2Does positive GEX predict lower next-day realized volatility, and negative GEX higher volatility and trendier moves?Volatility-regime hypothesis. Thousands of samples.
  3. RQ3Does a cross-strike attention model beat gradient-boosted trees and tabular neural networks trained on hand-built aggregates?Core deep learning question, answered with ablations.
  4. RQ4How do the models degrade across structural breaks: lot-size changes, the move of weekly expiries, and volatility regimes?Distribution-shift evaluation.
  5. RQ5Does a chain encoder trained on the data-rich volatility task transfer to the data-poor pinning task?Transfer learning to a low-sample target.

Cleaning

Rows are deduplicated per date, expiry, strike and option type and restricted to the 09:15–15:30 IST session. Truncated and special sessions are dropped. Strikes are kept only within about 8% of the forward (or delta 0.02–0.98) with minimum liquidity, because stale deep out-of-the-money quotes corrupt implied volatility and gamma. The forward price for each expiry is backed out from put–call parity rather than assumed, and settlement targets use NSE's official last-30-minute average rather than the last tick. The output is a single curated Parquet dataset with a schema document.

4Features and targets

Gamma exposure

GEXK = ΓK × OIK × lot size × S² × 0.01Rupee change in dealer hedge per 1% move in the index, at strike K. Greeks come from Black-76 on the synthetic forward.

Because nobody publishes who holds which options, GEX is computed under three sign conventions: naive (calls positive, puts negative, the S&P convention), absolute (all positive, measuring concentration only), and inferred (sign from open-interest changes combined with NSE's participant-wise positions). Comparing the three is itself a result.

Model inputs

Two views of the same option chain feed different models. The aggregate view summarises each snapshot as about 20 features and feeds LightGBM, the MLP and FT-Transformer. The token view keeps every liquid strike as its own vector and feeds the attention model.

6Evaluation

All models use the same expiry-aligned walk-forward protocol: training windows roll forward in time, with at least one expiry of embargo between train and test and no shuffling. Results are reported per walk and per regime.

  • Metrics: AUC and PR-AUC for classification, Spearman information coefficient for ranking, Brier score and reliability curves for calibration.
  • Distribution shift (RQ4): performance before versus after each lot-size change and the weekly-expiry weekday change, and across India VIX terciles. The gap between in-regime and cross-regime performance is the headline robustness number.
  • Interpretability: attention weights across strikes, checked against where the call wall, put wall and flip level sit.
  • Evaluation dashboard: a Streamlit app reads a results store written by the harness and shows walk-forward metrics, calibration by regime, and attention heatmaps.

AOptions primer: delta neutrality, gamma and GEX

This appendix is for readers without a finance background. It builds from single options to the gamma-exposure idea the project tests.

Options and the greeks

A call option pays off if the index finishes above its strike price; a put pays off if it finishes below. Nifty options are European and cash-settled: nothing is delivered, and the payout is computed from the official settlement price on expiry day. The greeks measure how an option's price responds to changes in its inputs.

  • Delta (Δ) is how much the option price moves for a one-point move in the index. Calls range from 0 to 1 and puts from −1 to 0; an at-the-money option sits near ±0.5.
  • Gamma (Γ) is how fast delta itself changes as the index moves. It is the same for a call and a put at the same strike, peaks at the money, and grows sharply as expiry approaches.
  • Theta (Θ) is the value an option loses each day from time passing.
  • Vega (ν) is sensitivity to implied volatility.

What delta-neutral means

A position is delta-neutral when its net delta is zero, so a small index move neither gains nor loses money to first order. A market maker who sells a customer 100 at-the-money calls is short about 50 deltas and would lose if the index rose. To neutralise that, the market maker buys futures worth about 50 deltas. The book is now indifferent to small moves and earns the spread instead of betting on direction.

The catch is gamma. As the index moves, the options' deltas change, so the hedge drifts out of balance and has to be rebalanced. The direction of that rebalancing is the whole mechanism behind GEX.

Dealer long gamma (+GEX)

When the index rises, the dealer's delta becomes positive, so they sell futures; when it falls, they buy. Hedging trades against the move, which damps volatility and can pull price toward heavily held strikes.

Dealer short gamma (−GEX)

When the index rises, the dealer's delta turns negative, so they buy futures; when it falls, they sell. Hedging trades with the move, which amplifies volatility and produces trendier days.

Delta-neutral strategies traders use

Delta-neutral positions are also deliberate strategies. A short straddle sells an at-the-money call and put together: it starts near zero delta, collects theta every day, and profits if the index stays near the strike. It is short gamma, so a large move in either direction hurts. A long straddle is the mirror image. Gamma scalping holds long options and repeatedly re-hedges delta, profiting when realized volatility exceeds the implied volatility paid. The project's optional economic check (section 10) uses a filtered expiry-day short straddle because pinning is exactly the condition under which it profits.

From one dealer to the whole market: GEX

Gamma exposure scales each strike's gamma by the open interest there, the lot size and the index level, giving the rupee amount dealers would need to trade for a 1% index move. Summed across strikes, the sign says whether dealers as a group are long or short gamma, and the size says how strong the hedging flow would be.

  • The flip level (zero-gamma level) is the index level at which total GEX changes sign, found by recomputing GEX across hypothetical index levels.
  • The call wall and put wall are the strikes with the largest call open interest above spot and put open interest below it. They often act as soft ceilings and floors.
  • Max pain is the strike at which the total payout to option holders at expiry would be smallest.
  • Pinning is the tendency for the index to settle near a large strike at expiry, plausibly because long-gamma hedging near expiry keeps pushing price back toward it.

Why the dealer's side is uncertain on NSE

The S&P convention assumes customers mostly sell calls (covered calls) and buy puts (protection), leaving dealers long calls and short puts. On NSE, retail traders are heavy buyers and sellers of both, and proprietary firms and foreign institutions take large positions of their own. That is why the project computes GEX under three sign conventions and uses NSE's daily participant-wise open interest, which splits long and short positions by client type, as the closest thing to ground truth.

Pricing details used in the pipeline

Greeks are computed with Black-76, a version of Black–Scholes that prices options off a forward price instead of spot. The forward for each expiry is backed out from put–call parity, which links a call and put at the same strike:

F = K + erT (C − P)Taken as the median across near-the-money strikes. This avoids having to estimate dividends and interest rates separately.

BGlossary

At the money (ATM)
A strike close to the current index level.
Bhavcopy
NSE's official end-of-day file of prices, volumes and open interest.
Implied volatility (IV)
The volatility implied by an option's market price; the market's forecast.
India VIX
NSE's volatility index, derived from Nifty option prices.
Lot size
The number of index units per contract; changed by NSE over time.
Moneyness
How far a strike is from the forward, here measured as log(K/F).
Open interest (OI)
The number of contracts outstanding at a strike.
Put–call ratio (PCR)
Put open interest or volume divided by call open interest or volume.
Realized volatility (RV)
Volatility actually observed, here the sum of squared 5-minute returns.
STT
Securities Transaction Tax, charged by India on option trades.
Variance risk premium
The typical gap between implied and subsequently realized volatility.
Walk-forward validation
Training on a past window and testing on the next, repeated forward in time, so no model sees the future.
